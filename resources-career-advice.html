Career advice

Where a holiday park career can take you, and how to get started.

Why work in holiday parks?

Holiday parks are some of the most varied workplaces in Australian tourism. In a single week you might check in guests, solve a maintenance problem, help plan a school holiday program and handle the books.

Many roles come with on-site accommodation, and parks are found everywhere from capital-city fringes to remote coastlines — so a park career can also be a lifestyle change.

Common career paths

  • Guest services and reception — a great entry point that teaches you how the whole park runs.
  • Housekeeping, maintenance and grounds — practical roles that are always in demand.
  • Supervisor and assistant manager — the step between team member and management.
  • Park manager and management couples — running the business day to day, often as a live-in role.
  • Operations, area and group roles — overseeing several parks for a group or brand.

Getting started

  1. Post your resume so hiring parks can find you.
  2. Set up job alerts for the categories and regions you're interested in.
  3. Consider seasonal or casual work to build experience quickly.
  4. Look into industry training for reservations, front office or management.

Tip: parks value reliability and a friendly attitude as much as experience. Say so, with examples, in your resume.
